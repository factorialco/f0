# F0SwipeDeck

Presents a queue one card at a time and asks for a binary decision on each:
drag right to accept, left to reject. The deck renders and animates; the
`useSwipeDeck` hook owns the queue, so the same decision can be driven by a
gesture, a button or a keyboard shortcut.

---

# Overview

## Anatomy

<Canvas of={Stories.Default} />

The deck is a labelled group holding one card at a time. The card is the
consumer's own content, rendered through `renderCard` — the deck adds only the
drag surface, the rotation as the card travels, and the exit animation.

## Guidelines

Pair `F0SwipeDeck` with `useSwipeDeck`. The hook is the source of truth for the
queue, so anything that decides a card — the gesture, an action button, a
shortcut — calls the same `decide`, `swipeLeft`, `swipeRight` and `undo`.

<Canvas of={Stories.DecidedWithoutDragging} />

A decision fires as the card leaves, not when the consumer's mutation resolves.
Pass the `rollback` given to `onDecide` to put the card back when that mutation
fails, and treat `onUndo` as the user changing their mind about a decision that
already happened.

Each item is decided exactly once, however fast the decisions arrive, so a
double-tap or a fast pair of swipes can never skip a card silently.

## When not to use

- The user needs to compare items, or revisit them out of order: use a list or
  a table, where everything stays on screen.
- There are more than two outcomes per item, or the outcome needs a form.
- The queue is long enough that reviewing it card by card would be a chore.

<Canvas of={Stories.Empty} />

## Accessibility

The deck renders as a labelled group, named through the `label` prop or the
`swipeDeck.label` translation. Because a drag is not an accessible control, the
decisions must also be reachable from buttons wired to the same hook — the
deck deliberately ships no gesture-only path. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`
the card neither rotates nor animates out.
