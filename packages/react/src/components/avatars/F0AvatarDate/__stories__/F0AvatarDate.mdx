# AvatarDate

The avatar for a date, shown as a compact calendar chip.

---

## Overview

### Purpose

- Represent a date — an event, deadline or milestone — at a glance
- Give date-bound items a recognizable, consistent marker in lists and cards

### Anatomy

A date avatar stacks the abbreviated month over the day number in a compact
chip. Because it conveys the date as text rather than an image or icon, it
renders at a single fixed size instead of the shared size scale: at the smaller
sizes the month and day would become too small to stay legible.

<Canvas of={DateStories.Default} />

<Controls of={DateStories.Default} />

Both values are read off `date` in the viewer's local timezone, so the chip
shows the day the viewer is in rather than the UTC one. The box is the same 40px
square for every date, so a single-digit day and a double-digit one occupy
identical space and a column of chips stays aligned.

## Guidelines

### Design best practices

The chip is a marker, not a full date. Put it next to the text that names the
item, and let that text carry the parts the chip leaves out — the year, the
weekday, the time.

<Canvas of={DateStories.InContext} />

#### When to use

- Marking events, deadlines or other date-stamped items
- Calendars, timelines and scheduling surfaces

#### When NOT to use

- Representing a person, team or other entity: use `F0Avatar` or the matching
  `F0Avatar*` component
- The date is the primary content of the row: render it as text, where it can be
  formatted in full and localized
- The year matters — an archive, a multi-year history, anything not obviously
  "this year": the chip cannot show it

## Accessibility

The month and the day are real text, so a screen reader reaches them with no
extra markup. What it reads is `"Dec"` and then `"13"` as two separate nodes —
not a date. Everything below follows from that.

- **The full date has to exist as text next to the chip.** "Dec 13" says nothing
  about the year, or about what the date refers to.
- **There is no machine-readable date.** The chip renders plain `<div>`s, not
  `<time dateTime="…">`, so nothing can parse or re-format what it shows.

### Known limitations

Neither of these is fixable from the consumer side; both are noted so you don't
build on them.

- **`aria-label` and `aria-labelledby` are accepted but do not work.** They land
  on the root `<div>`, which carries no `role`. `aria-label` on a role-less
  element is an ARIA-prohibited attribute: assistive technology is not required
  to honour it. Because the chip has text content, axe reports it as needs-review
  (`aria-prohibited-attr`, WCAG 4.1.2) rather than a violation, so no automated
  gate catches it. Don't pass either prop — the surrounding text is what works.
- **The month text sits on the 4.5:1 line.** In light mode it is 12px semibold
  `f1-special-highlight` on `f1-background-inverse-secondary`, which resolves to
  4.59:1 over the default page background: a pass by 0.09. On a tinted surface it
  drops below the threshold — over `f1-background-tertiary` it is 4.45:1, a WCAG
  AA failure. Keep the chip on the default background.
